## The phone as a remote for the built-in browser

A cabinet often has neither keyboard nor mouse, yet "Récupérer" (Get) asks you to sign in to community sites and click around them.
The banner shows a QR code, **"Télécommande" (Remote)**: the phone, on the same Wi-Fi, opens a page served by the cabinet itself (no external server, nothing to install).
It offers a **touchpad** (swipe: the pointer; tap: a click; two taps close together: a double click; two fingers: scrolling; long press or two-finger tap: right-click), **the phone's keyboard** as soon as a field of the page has the keyboard focus, and **Back, Forward, Keyboard, More, Close**.
First milestone: the built-in browser only.

- **Pairing** (`packages/core/src/remote.ts`, `createRemotePairing`): the QR carries the page address and a 256-bit key **in the fragment** (`http://<cabinet>:<port>/#k=…`), which a browser never sends over the network.
  It is valid for three minutes (`REMOTE_PAIRING_MS`), then renews itself, and serves a single phone.
  The phone that read it shows a four-digit code, the banner the same one ("Même code ?", Same code?): **Start or "Accepter" (Accept) accepts it, Exit or "Refuser" (Refuse) refuses it**.
  The banner then takes the keyboard focus, with "Accepter" selected: Enter confirms the selected button, the arrow keys move from one to the other (real test on 06/11/2026: the page kept the keyboard focus, the mouse was needed).
  With no answer within a minute (`REMOTE_ACCEPT_MS`), it is refused.
  Once accepted, it receives a session key, which lets it come back after a break (page reloaded, screen locked, Wi-Fi lost for a moment) without being accepted again.
  **One phone at a time**: the QR disappears while a phone waits or drives; "Déconnecter" (Disconnect, banner or phone) shows a new one, with another key.
- **Encryption**: the page is served over HTTP (a certificate from the cabinet would make the phone show a security warning), where the browser offers no WebCrypto.
  Each message is therefore sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 (RFC 8439), written in JavaScript for the phone (`app/renderer/telephone/telephone-core.js`) and in TypeScript for the cabinet (`packages/core/src/chacha.ts`): Electron ships BoringSSL, whose `createCipheriv` does not know this algorithm.
  The associated data bind each message to its connection (the other side's challenge) and an increasing number to its rank: a message replayed, from another connection or under another key cuts the link.
  Nobody on the Wi-Fi reads what is typed or drives the cabinet without the QR's key.
  Limit: the page itself travels in the clear; someone able to alter the Wi-Fi traffic at the moment it is opened could change it.
- **Server** (`app/src/phone-remote.ts`): it starts when a QR code is shown ("Récupérer"'s banner, the remote screen, the on-screen keyboard: see [The phone as a remote for the menu](the-phone-as-a-remote-for-the-menu.md#the-phone-as-a-remote-for-the-menu)) and stops when no QR is shown and no phone is connected any more (listening, link, keys).
  It serves only the phone page, its style sheets, its scripts, the font and the language catalogue (`/catalogue.js`), with a strict security policy (`default-src 'none'`), no cache and no referrer; it refuses any address outside the local network (`isLocalNetworkAddress`: 10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16, link-local, the machine itself), any host name other than the cabinet's addresses (DNS rebinding), any origin other than its own, more than four connections, a frame over 4 KB.
  Link: a minimal WebSocket with no dependency (`packages/core/src/websocket.ts`), a ping every ten seconds.
- **Network and firewall**: the QR's address is the private IPv4 of a real adapter (Wi-Fi, Ethernet; 192.168 first, never a virtual adapter, `pickLanAddress`).
  The "Cyber Pinball" rule of the Windows firewall targets `CyberPinball.exe` for every port (`firewall.ts`): the server, in the same process, benefits from it, with no extra rule and no prompt.
  **Without it, nothing listens**: Windows would show its prompt, which nobody can confirm at the cabinet, and would add blocking rules that would also cut the friends network.
  The block then stays visible, greyed out with its reason ("pare-feu de Windows à autoriser" (allow it in the Windows firewall), "aucun réseau local" (no local network), "lancé depuis les sources" (run from source)).
  During a test (simulated or disabled network, unattended test: `phoneRemoteMode`), 127.0.0.1 only; `CYBERPINBALL_RESEAU=non` turns it off.
- **Injection**: the phone drives the whole window, the site page **and the banner** (Web, Images, YouTube, Video engines, search field, buttons: real test on 06/11/2026, the engine could not be changed).
  Its gestures arrive where the pointer is, like those of a mouse and keyboard: `sendInputEvent` (moves, clicks, Enter, Tab, Backspace and arrow keys), and text one character at a time, as `char` events (accents, emojis: `typeChars`, `app/src/page-input.ts`); text and keys go where the phone last clicked (page or banner).
  Never `insertText`: it crashes the page's renderer (access violation) as soon as no element has focus in it, after Tab or when the page moves focus between two steps, as Google's does; that was the crash of 10/11/2026 on the PinCab, and its promise never settled, which blocked the queue of gestures.
  A gesture gives control back after 3 s at most, a gesture that fails is logged in `erreurs.txt` and dropped, and a page that crashes anyway is logged, reloaded once, the keyboard going to the banner where Esc and Exit always close.
  Safeguard and test bench: `packages/core/src/test/saisie-robuste.test.ts` (no `insertText` in the application; an off-screen bench, on local pages that mimic an email field with passkeys, a field replaced while typing, a navigation to another site in the middle of a burst, a cross-origin frame removed, a suggestion list, a page that crashes, a window closed during a burst).
  Two-finger scrolling always goes to the page (the banner does not scroll), preceded by a move on the spot.
  A phone that drives accepts nobody: while it drives, another phone is refused outright and the banner has neither "Accepter" nor "Refuser".
  What the phone may ask for is closed (`parsePhoneMessage`): never Escape, nor a function key, nor anything but the planned gestures.
- **Pointer**: the Windows pointer does not move; the phone's pointer is drawn in a transparent window laid over the whole "Récupérer" window (`app/renderer/remote-pointer.*`, `overlayContent` in `cover-display.ts`): without focus, without a taskbar button, attached to the window (above it, minimised and closed with it), and let through by clicks and by the cabinet's mouse.
  It glides at each screen frame towards its new place (damped pursuit, 26 ms time constant): Wi-Fi delivers the gestures in bursts, and a small view moved at each gesture advanced in jerks (real test on 06/11/2026).
  It follows the finger with acceleration (`movePointer`: slowly, a swipe from one edge of the pad to the other crosses a third of the screen; quickly, the whole screen), computed on a smoothed speed of the latest gestures (`smoothSpeed`), and a ring leaves the tip on each click.
  Frames go out without waiting (`setNoDelay`).
- **Keyboard**: the `REMOTE_FOCUS_PROBE` probe, run in every frame of the page (or of the banner) after a click, a key, a page load and every 0.7 s, tells whether an input field has the keyboard focus, its kind (text, password, email, number, address…), its label and **its text, which the phone takes over** so it can be corrected (real test on 06/11/2026: a filled field arrived empty).
  **Never the text of a password**, nor of a field the site marks as secret (`autocomplete`: card `cc-…`, one-time code, password shown in clear), nor of a rich area (`contentEditable`), nor a text over 512 characters.
  The phone then opens its panel: the field's label, a field of the same type (hidden for a password, without autocorrect), Backspace, "Champ suivant" (Next field, Tab), Enter.
  Field taken over: each change sends the whole text (`set` message), which the cabinet puts in place of the field's text (Ctrl+A, then the text), wherever it was corrected; the text is only sent back to the phone when the field changes (click, Tab, Enter, another field), never while typing in it.
  Field not taken over: what changes is sent as deletions then additions at the end (`textDiff`), the keyboard's corrections replace the last word.
  Nothing typed or taken over goes into the log; Cyber Pinball writes nothing into passwords or cookies: the user types, into the site's page.
- **Right-click**: the Windows menu could not be reached from the phone; a right-click from the phone therefore sends the page menu's actions to the phone ("Ouvrir le lien" (Open the link), "Télécharger cette image" (Download this image) in free search).
  The **"Plus" (More)** sheet offers what the banner offers, easier with a finger than with the pointer: "Retour à la fiche" (Back to the file page, or to the search), "Recharger la page" (Reload the page), "Télécharger cette vidéo" (Download this video), "Récupérer le son / la vidéo" (Get the sound / the video), "Annuler : <file>" (Cancel: <file>), the free search with its engines (Web, Images, YouTube, Video: another engine reruns the same text at once, as in the banner), "Déconnecter ce téléphone" (Disconnect this phone).
  **Close** asks for confirmation, then closes the built-in browser: the phone goes back to the menu buttons.
- **Phone page** (`app/renderer/telephone/*`): the cabinet's theme, text of at least 15 px, 48 px targets; the page follows the visible area when the phone's keyboard opens (`visualViewport`, `interactive-widget=resizes-content`); each state has its screen (connecting, codes to compare, reconnecting, refused, QR expired or already used, another phone connected, disconnected, browser closed).
- **Log** (`data\logs\journal.txt`): listening and address, phone reading the QR (its name, "Android · Chrome"), accepted, refused, disconnected, link cut and why (unreadable or replayed frame, refused request), QR expired or already used; never a typed text, a key or a page address.
- **Tests**: `packages/core/src/test/remote.test.ts` (encryption on both sides compared with `node:crypto` and the RFC vectors, pairing, addresses, messages, pointer, typing, WebSocket, QR decoded by jsQR); `packages/core/src/test/telecommande.test.ts` runs the remote control scenario end to end in a real off-screen Chromium (simulated phone: the real phone page, which reads the QR, is accepted by Start, drives, types, takes over and corrects a filled field but never a password, opens a link by a long press, clicks in the banner, disconnects; the drawn arrow catches up with the tip; then another host, another origin, QR already used, Exit, "Accepter" with Enter and the arrow keys, forbidden key, replayed frame, unknown key, Close) and pins the invariants (only `social-net.ts` and `phone-remote.ts` listen; no listening on the network without the firewall rule; no `createCipheriv` on the cabinet side).
- **Second milestone**: the same link for the whole menu, next section.
